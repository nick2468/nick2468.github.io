My son drew the screen for this one on a sheet of paper: a cube, a row of colours, and a
box where "the computer types in what to do". Cuber is that drawing, built. You hold your
scrambled cube, paint its colours on to the cube on the screen by clicking (or by a
right-click menu, or a long press on a touch screen), then turn the screen cube round to
do the other three faces. The colours are checked as you go, so a sticker that cannot be
where you put it is explained rather than attempted.

Press Solve and the moves appear in the box, each as the standard letter and a plain
sentence: "R' - Turn the right face anticlockwise." Step through them, or press Play, and
the cube on the screen turns with each move so you can follow along on the real one.
Hint gives you one move at a time instead.

There are three ways to solve, chosen in the box and remembered. Quick solve finds about
twenty moves. Fewest moves looks for the shortest answer the same solver can find, so a
cube you scrambled with three turns comes back as those three turns undone. The
beginner method is the layer-by-layer method every guide teaches, about 170 moves in
seven named stages, each stage explained and each well-known sequence named as it is
used, so you learn the method rather than just following it.

The installer is not signed with a code signing certificate, so Windows warns that the
publisher is unknown and asks whether to continue. That warning is expected.
